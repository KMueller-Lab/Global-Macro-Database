## Overview

The 2026-06 quarterly update expands coverage with thirty nine new aggregator and country-level historical sources, improves our continuous automated error-monitoring system to improve data quality, resolves dozens of data-quality bugs spanning units, currency redenominations, and series splicing, completes a comprehensive audit of the Mitchell historical statistics, and improves the Stata, R, and Python packages. The entire release is produced by the GMD pipeline running unfiltered, end-to-end, on cloud infrastructure.

## New Sources

This release adds thirty nine new sources, bringing the total in the database to 160 (up from 121 at the previous release). The additions are dominated by long-run country-level historical sources, complemented by several new cross-country aggregators. Highlights include:

- \citet{WB_GEM}: the World Bank Global Economic Monitor, providing global macroeconomic indicators.
- \citet{SECMCA_HIST}: historical macroeconomic statistics from the Central American Monetary Council (SECMCA) for Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, and El Salvador (1960--2017).
- \citet{CS7_DEU}: German interwar and pre-WWII macroeconomic series compiled from primary historical sources.
- \citet{CS1_MEX}: the Archivo de Historia Econ\'omica de M\'exico --- long-run Mexican national accounts, prices, money, and public finances (1876--2025).
- \citet{CS1_GHA}: historical monetary, inflation, and output series for Ghana (1980--2017).
- \citet{CS3_AUS}: Foster's Australian Economic Statistics (1949/50--1994/95).
- \citet{CS3_NLD}: the Herengracht house-price index (1628--1973), one of the longest continuous real-estate price series available.
- \citet{CS3_KOR}: Open Fiscal Data from the Republic of Korea's Ministry of Economy and Finance.
- \citet{CS3_CHN}: Chinese national income estimates, 1661--1933.
- \citet{CS3_GBR}: Feinstein's National Income, Expenditure and Output of the United Kingdom (1855--1965).
- \citet{CS4_GBR}: the Sefton--Weale balanced estimates of UK national income (1920--1990).

Beyond these highlights, the release adds many further long-run country-level historical sources --- including new slots for Germany (six new historical series), the United Kingdom, India, New Zealand, the Netherlands, Australia, Spain, Sweden, Turkey, Japan, Russia, Israel, Bulgaria, and Venezuela --- alongside additional cross-country aggregators.

In addition, the CEPII trade-and-macro source (\citet{CEPII}, two centuries of bilateral trade and gravity data, 1827--2014) has been refreshed and renamed from \texttt{CEPII\_TRADHIST} to \texttt{CEPII}.

## Extended Error Monitoring

This release extends our continuous error-monitoring system that automatically scans the database for ratio discrepancies, unit breaks, and other anomalies, and routes each flagged case through an auto-triage workflow for review. 

## Data Quality and Bug Fixes

Driven in large part by the improved monitoring system and by reports from our community of contributors, this release resolves dozens of bugs. The most impactful corrections fall into two groups:

- **Units, scaling, and redenominations.** We corrected unit-scale and currency-redenomination errors across many sources, including the IMF IFS, IMF GFS, Andersson, the Bordo monetary aggregates, Flora, the African Development Bank source, HFS, and the United Nations.
- **Splicing and ratio consistency.** We improved the handling of gaps between sources, and corrected the US long-term interest-rate series.

## Mitchell Historical Audit

We completed a comprehensive audit of the Mitchell International Historical Statistics, fixing issues and correcting the euro-cutoff handling for European government tax series.

## Easier Download of Underlying Raw Data

Previously, users could only access the raw data underlying the spliced GMD series by either downloading the Excel file or using our Stata, R, or Python packages. From this version onwards, users can also directly include all underlying raw data when downloading the csv or Stata versions. 

## Package Updates

The Stata, R, and Python packages have all been improved in this release. The three distributions remain at full feature parity, so every user has the same access to the combined database, the underlying source data, and the documentation regardless of which language they work in.

## Documentation and License

We substantially expanded the Technical Appendix. It now includes a clarified license and a comprehensive Frequently Asked Questions section. A redesigned set of per-variable and per-country PDF documentation make it easier to trace where the data are taken from.
