## Overview

The 2026_09 release focuses on data quality and coverage. It corrects hundreds of errors in exchange rates, currency units, and government deficits, and adds seven sources, including historical estimates that take nominal GDP for Spain, Italy, and Portugal back to 1277, 1310, and 1527.

## New Sources

This release adds seven sources, bringing the total to 167.

- \textbf{Prados de la Escosura, Álvarez-Nogal and Santiago-Caballero}: annual estimates for preindustrial Spain, 1277–1850, published by the Fundación Rafael del Pino. Spanish nominal GDP now begins in 1277 instead of 1827. The database keeps its existing real GDP and population for Spain.
- \textbf{Malanima}: GDP for central and northern Italy, 1310–1913. Italian nominal GDP now begins in 1310 instead of 1861, real GDP in 1310 instead of 1803, and the consumer price index in 1310 instead of 1549, with no gap between 1592 and 1799.
- \textbf{Palma and Reis}: a reconstruction of Portuguese economic growth, 1527–1850. It takes real GDP back from 1801 to 1527, nominal GDP from 1827 to 1527, and population from 1800 to 1527; the database already had population for 1600 and 1700. Consumer prices now begin in 1527 instead of 1549, and the gaps in the series up to 1671 are filled.
- \textbf{Sultan Nazrin Shah}: expenditure-based national accounts for Malaya, 1900–1939, published by the Economic History of Malaysia project. They are the database's first estimates of nominal GDP, household and government consumption, fixed investment, and inflation for Malaysia before the Second World War. They also replace the earlier real GDP for these years and add exports and imports for 1900–1911, which the trade sources did not cover.
- \textbf{Catão and Solomou}: trade-weighted real effective exchange rates for sixteen countries, 1870–1913. For Argentina, Brazil, Chile, China, India, Japan, Mexico, and the United States, these are the first real effective exchange rates before the First World War. For Greece they add 1870–1876; for the other seven countries the database keeps its existing series.
- \textbf{Bank of Canada and Bank of England}: sovereign default data for 174 countries, 1960–2024, from the BoC–BoE Sovereign Default Database. They add 101 sovereign debt crises in 64 countries. Three existing crises move one or two years earlier: Ghana from 1966 to 1965, Peru from 1969 to 1968, and India from 1969 to 1967.
- \textbf{Asian Development Bank}: an archived version of the Key Indicators Database, which keeps series that the current version no longer provides.

## Updated Sources

- \textbf{Laeven and Valencia}: banking crisis dates now come from the latest edition of the systemic banking crises database. It extends coverage from 2017 to 2025 and adds thirteen crises.

## Use with AI agents

The Global Macro Database can now be used directly from AI agents through the \href{https://mcp.anansidata.com/}{Anansi MCP server}, which works with any client that supports the Model Context Protocol. For coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex, the open-source \href{https://github.com/AnansiDataAnalytics/anansidata-agent-plugin}{Anansi Data plugin} sets up the connection. After signing in with an Anansi account, an agent can search the series, retrieve observations with their sources, compare and rank countries, and analyse and plot the data.
